How much should you save each week to hit your goal?

Work out your number, then watch the weeks add up.

one small square a week, until the date you picked

A savings goal can feel huge until you break it into weeks. £500 sounds like a lot to find all at once. £50 a week for ten weeks sounds like something you can actually do without much fuss.

Most goals fall apart not because the total is unreasonable, but because nobody worked out what it actually means week to week. A number with no shape attached to it is easy to put off; a number that fits into an ordinary week is much easier to just start.

The maths behind it is simple. You need three things: how much you're saving for, how much (if anything) you've already put aside, and the date you'd like to reach it by. Everything else follows from those three: the weekly number, and the weeks left to get there.

Say the goal is £500 and the date is ten weeks away, with nothing saved yet, that works out to £50 a week. Add in £120 already saved and the number drops to £38 a week for the same target. The date and the starting point matter just as much as the total.

Weekly works well because it matches how a lot of budgeting already happens: a bit put aside from what's left over, checked in on every few days rather than once a month. It's also just easier to picture: setting something aside for one week ahead is a far smaller ask than staring down a target that's months away.

No fixed date yet? Pick a rough one anyway. Even a loose target date is enough to turn "save more" into an actual number, and it's easy to adjust later if life moves the goalposts.

None of this needs to be complicated. It's four numbers and one division sum: the kind of maths that takes longer to explain than it does to actually do. Here's the whole thing laid out, step by step, no calculator required (though there's one below if you'd rather skip straight to the answer):

Finding your number

  1. 1Write down your goal amount and today's date, so you've got a clear starting point to work from.
  2. 2Decide on the date you'd like to reach your goal by: something realistic, not just hopeful.
  3. 3Count the number of weeks between today and that date, rounding to the nearest whole week.
  4. 4Subtract anything you've already saved so far from the total goal amount.
  5. 5Divide what's left by the number of weeks. That's roughly what to aim for each week.
worth considering The number you land on doesn't need to be exact to be useful. Round it to something that fits comfortably into your week, and let a slightly bigger week make up for a smaller one later on if you need to. A rough weekly number you actually stick to beats a precise one that never gets looked at again.

When you want to raid the savings jar

A few small moves for when the urge to dip into the savings strikes.

Wanting to dip into a goal you're actively saving for is completely normal. It tends to occur somewhere in the middle, once the excitement of starting to save has worn off, but the finish line still feels far away, and it can happen whether this is the first time you've felt it on this goal or the fifth.

Say you've been putting £20 a week into a holiday fund, an emergency buffer, or something you've wanted for ages. Then something else catches your eye, and suddenly you'd quite like that £60 for it instead. Nothing about the original goal has changed, but for a moment the new thing feels a lot more real than it does.

Taking £60 out might feel like a small decision today, but if you're saving £20 a week, it adds three weeks onto your goal. Save less each week and it adds even more. The urge to spend usually fades within the hour. The extra three weeks stay on the goal long after that.

Taking money out is sometimes the right call. Something else can genuinely matter more in the moment, and there's nothing wrong with deciding that. What's worth aiming for is making that choice deliberately, instead of letting a passing urge decide it for you.

Strategies to try

A few small checks can help you tell a genuine change of mind from a passing urge, without having to work it out from scratch every time it shows up.

Name it
Write down exactly what you want to take the money out for, and why you want it today.
Check the cost
Work out how much longer it'll take to reach your goal if you take the money out.
Ask future you
When you finally reach the goal, will you be glad you waited, or glad you spent the money instead?
Slow it down
Still want to take it out after all that? Give yourself a full day before you do.

The trade, laid out

Sometimes it helps to put the two sides of the decision next to each other, rather than only feeling one of them at a time.

What you get now
  • The thing you're tempted to spend the money on.
  • The relief of not having to wait for it.
What your goal gets
  • A few more weeks added before you reach your goal.
  • Another stretch of saving to get back to where you were.

Neither choice is right or wrong on its own. You're deciding whether what you want today is worth a little more time on your original goal, and that's a call only you can make, based on what's actually in front of you right now.

worth considering Everything you've already saved stays saved if you dip in. You're not starting from zero. You're only adding a little more ground still to cover before you reach the goal.

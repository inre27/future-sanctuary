Your Payday Bill Checklist

Whatever your pay schedule, see which bills are safely covered — and which ones cut it close.

Some bills land with days of breathing room after payday. Others land close enough that there's barely a gap, and it's not always the amount you'd expect. Say you're paid every two weeks and your last payday was Friday 5 June: a bill due 8 June has eleven days of cushion before the next payday on 19 June. A bill due 15 June has just four. Same paycheck, same cycle, but a very different amount of room. Whatever your pay schedule, the same quick check tells you which is which.

Why this happens

A 7- or 14-day pay cycle doesn't divide evenly into a year, so it slides against any bill fixed to a calendar date. It's the same drift that gives biweekly pay an extra paycheck twice a year. Paid weekly or every two weeks, a bill that's tight this cycle can ease up in a few months, and one that's comfortable now can tighten later. Paid monthly or twice a month, your payday and the bill's date move together, so the gap tends to stay much the same cycle to cycle.

Finding your at-risk bills

  1. 1List your recurring bills: the ones that repeat every cycle, not a one-off payment.
  2. 2Rank them by size. The bill worth checking first is your biggest one, not your most frequent one.
  3. 3For each of your biggest bills, work out which payday it falls after and how many days are left until the next one (the tool below does this instantly).
  4. 4Any big bill landing within a handful of days of your next payday goes on your at-risk list.
  5. 5Small recurring charges, like a streaming subscription or a phone top-up, rarely need this level of attention. The amount at stake is too small to matter much either way.
worth remembering A short recheck of your biggest bills every few months keeps you ahead of surprises, without turning bill-tracking into an ongoing chore.

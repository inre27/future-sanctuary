Which Paycheck Actually Covers This Bill?

Your biweekly payday shifts, but your bill's due date doesn't. Find out which paycheck actually covers it before it's due.

Paid every two weeks, your payday shifts a little earlier against the calendar each cycle. Most bills don't move at all, so the same bill can be comfortably funded by one paycheck, then land uncomfortably close to the next one a few months later.

A paycheck every 14 days doesn't line up evenly with a calendar month, so your payday's date drifts against any bill that's fixed to a particular day (rent on the 1st, say). Most cycles, that drift is small enough that a bill lands safely soon after payday or safely with plenty of time before the next one. Other cycles, it lands close enough to the next payday that there's almost nothing left in between. A bill due somewhere in the middle usually has enough of a cushion either way, but it's still worth checking once.

For example, say your last payday was Friday 5 June and a bill is due 15 June, 10 days later. That paycheck covers it, but only just: there are 4 days left until the next one lands on 19 June. A bill due earlier in the same stretch, say 8 June, tells a different story: 3 days after payday, with 11 days still to go. Same payday, same 14-day cycle, very different amount of breathing room depending on where the due date falls.

How to work it out

Here's how to check any bill by hand, no tools needed:

  1. 1Write down your last payday and the due date of the bill you're checking.
  2. 2From your last payday, count forward in 14-day jumps until the next jump would land after the bill's due date.
  3. 3Stop on the payday right before that jump. That's the one covering the bill.
  4. 4Count how many days after that payday the bill falls.
  5. 510 days or more? Treat it as a late-cycle bill and check your balance before it's due, not after.
worth considering Checking a handful of your biggest recurring bills, like rent, a car payment or insurance, is usually enough; the smaller ones rarely catch you off guard. The safe zone shifts across the year too: a bill fixed to the same date every month drifts in and out of the risky zone, the same way the extra-paycheck months do. It's worth rechecking your biggest bills every couple of months, not just the first time.

📅 For biweekly pay

Your Extra Paycheck Months

Twice a year, biweekly pay squeezes a third paycheck into one calendar month. Find both before they land.

Paid every two weeks? You get 26 paychecks a year, not 24, which means twice a year you get a third paycheck instead of the usual two.

A paycheck every 14 days doesn't line up evenly with a 30-day month, so the two drift in and out of step across the year. Most months land two paychecks, like usual. Twice a year, that drift lines up so a third paycheck falls in the same month.

For example, say your last payday was Friday 2 January. The next lands two weeks later, then two weeks after that, all the way through the year. Line those dates up against a calendar and most months catch two of them. Every so often, three land inside the same 30 or 31 days instead, and that's your extra-paycheck month.

Most budgets don't have anywhere to put it. Bills are usually planned against the calendar month (rent due on the 1st, whether or not that's your payday), while biweekly pay runs on its own separate rhythm underneath. There's no column for the extra one, so it often gets spent on ordinary things before anyone notices it was extra.

How to find yours

Here's how to work out your own extra-paycheck months by hand, no tools needed:

  1. 1Grab a calendar (paper or digital) and mark today's date, plus your next payday.
  2. 2From that payday, count forward 14 days and mark the next one. Keep going in 14-day jumps for the rest of the year.
  3. 3Look at each calendar month on its own. Most will have exactly two paydays marked.
  4. 4Twice across the year, one month will have three marked paydays instead of two, and that's an extra-paycheck month.
  5. 5Once you've found both months, decide in advance what each extra paycheck is for: debt, savings, a buffer, or something just for you.
worth considering Deciding today, even loosely, beats spending it without noticing and wondering where it went. There's no single right answer here: send it straight at debt, treat it as the one paycheck in the year that isn't already spoken for, or split it between a few things.

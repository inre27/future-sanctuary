Before You Spend That Payment

Split every payment the same way the moment it lands, so your bills are covered before you spend on anything else.

A payment lands with no fixed date to plan the next one around, and it's easy to spend before deciding what it needs to cover. Say £850 comes in: set aside 20% for tax first, that's £170, leaving £680. £400 of bills are due before the next payment, leaving £280. Set aside 10% of that for savings, £28, and £252 is genuinely free to spend.

Why this works better than a monthly budget

A monthly budget assumes money that hasn't arrived yet. With a fixed payday, that's usually a safe assumption. Without one, it means planning around income you can't be sure is coming, or when. Splitting each payment the same way as it lands keeps your bills covered by money that's already there.

Not every bill needs the same attention. A streaming subscription landing awkwardly probably won't have too much impact. Rent, a loan repayment, or anything else due before you're likely to be paid again does. Sorting bills into essential and flexible once means the split above only has to account for the ones that would actually cause a problem if missed.

  1. 1Sort your recurring bills into essential (must-pay every time) and flexible (can wait a cycle) using the worksheet below. Worth redoing if your bills change, but not before every payment.
  2. 2Add up your essential bills total. That's the number you'll use every time a payment lands.
  3. 3When a payment arrives, set aside your tax percentage first. It's owed regardless, so it comes off before anything else.
  4. 4Subtract your essential bills total from what's left after tax.
  5. 5Set aside your savings percentage from whatever's left after bills.
  6. 6What's left after that is free to spend, or put toward a flexible bill.
worth remembering Doing the split every time matters more than getting the percentages exactly right. A rough split decided the moment a payment lands beats a precise one worked out after some of it's already gone.

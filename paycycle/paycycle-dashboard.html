paycheck coverage checker ⓘeach upcoming paycheck, with the bills that fall due before the next paycheck listed under it.
  • use a bill's dropdown to move it to a different paycheck
  • essential bills are checked against each paycheck separately, so you can see if one won't stretch
  • card and loan bills are listed but not counted in the totals; card payment bills are

beat the clock ⓘfor cards with a 0% offer, works out what to pay each month to clear the balance before the offer ends.
  • the card whose 0% ends first is at the top
  • monthly amount = balance divided by the whole months left
  • if you miss it, interest starts on whatever is left at the card's rate
use it to pick which card to put extra money on, ahead of the avalanche or snowball order.

balance forecast ⓘwhere your bank balance is heading, day by day.
  • starts from today's balance
  • takes off unpaid bills, card payments and transfers out
  • adds expected paychecks (▲)
  • the shaded area is overdrawn
move along the chart (or touch and drag) to see what comes off or lands on any day. with several current accounts, paychecks are assumed to land in each one shown.
income vs bills by month ⓘpaired bars for the last 3 months and the next 2; lighter bars are expected months. the figure under each month is income minus bills. income is paychecks you logged plus ones still due.
pay-week pinch map ⓘwhich weeks your bills bunch up, and whether paydays land before or after them. weeks run monday to sunday; hover a week to see its bills.
where each paycheck goes ⓘsplits each payday-to-payday stretch into bills, card payments, moves between accounts, logged spending and what's left. bills charged to a card aren't counted; the card payment is. spending only shows for periods that have started.
runway & steady income ⓘ
  • runway: how many days your bank balance covers your essential bills if no money came in
  • safe amount: the lowest recent paycheck from each dated source, scaled to a month. anything above it is a bonus
sources with no schedule and no logged paychecks aren't counted. bills are averaged over 12 months, so quarterly and yearly ones count.
how locked-in you are ⓘyour bills split into essential, important and flexible (set on each bill), as a percentage of your typical monthly income. the more that's essential, the less room you have to cut back.
big bills coming up ⓘquarterly and yearly bills over the next 12 months, and how much to set aside each month so none is a shock.
money flow ⓘincome against spending across everything you've logged. income includes paychecks you logged in settings, so don't also add the same paycheck as a transaction or it counts twice. transfers and balance adjustments are left out.
spending by month ⓘwhat you logged as spending in each of the last 6 months. the dashed line is the average. transfers and balance corrections aren't counted.

  • how much of each limit you're using (under 30% is the usual goal)

debt-free path ⓘ
  • solid line: your actual total debt, rebuilt from your transaction log
  • dashed lines: projections from today, one paying only minimums and one with your chosen method and extra payment
projections use each card's APR and ignore 0% offers, the same as the debt planner, so the dates match.
net position ⓘbank balances (current and savings) minus card and loan debt, over time. past points are rebuilt by taking your logged transactions back out of today's balances, so they're only as complete as your log.
tracking accuracy ⓘeach time you correct an account balance, PayCycle logs the difference as an adjustment. these bars total them by month. big or frequent adjustments mean PayCycle has drifted from your bank (missing transactions, wrong amounts); small ones mean it matches closely.
